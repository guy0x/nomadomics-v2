## Distinctive Voice Markers

### Opening patterns (verbatim from corpus)

1. **Scene-setting vignettes** — paint a picture before the thesis:
   - *"Picture this: while your former colleagues are stuck in rush hour traffic heading to their fluorescent-lit offices, you're wrapping up your workday on a sunlit balcony overlooking the Adriatic Sea."*
   - *"Imagine this: You're in Thailand, sipping a coconut on the beach, and you need to pay your freelancer in Germany."*
   - *"But while you're chasing sunsets and Wi-Fi signals, there's one thing you can't outrun: taxes."*

2. **The "carrot" frame** — open with the savings/benefit number:
   - *"If you could cut that by 20% using a few easy hacks, you're saving over $900 per year…"*
   - *"That means hundreds — sometimes thousands — of dollars stay in your pocket every year."*

3. **Pattern-break into reality check** — dream → wake-up moment:
   - *"Suddenly, you wake up. You're no longer in Thailand, but you're in your apartment…"* (crypto piece)

### Tone & punctuation

- **Contractions** — always used ("you're", "won't", "can't", "it's").
- **Em-dashes** — frequent, often as parenthetical pivots: *"…defying all conventional financial wisdom— you're saving more money than ever."*
- **Dashes for snark** — *"failing to comply can lead to hefty fines, double taxation, or even legal trouble"*.
- **Parenthetical asides** — color commentary: *"(or baht, or lari, or whatever currency gives you more bang for your buck)"*.
- **Occasional mild profanity / edgy imagery** — *"stick a fat middle finger to airlines' carry-on allowances"*. Use sparingly; never gratuitous.
- **Sarcasm toward institutions** — *"The Man"*, *"The taxman"*, *"schools don't teach"*. Targets: airlines, tax authorities, banks. Never the reader.

### Vocabulary register

- **Money-first framing** — always lead with $/€/%, never abstract.
- **Concrete numbers** — *"$140 round-trip"*, *"€15,000"*, *"22 x 14 x 9 inches"* — vague numbers kill credibility.
- **Tool-name drops** — brand names are part of the texture (Wise, Revolut, Chase Sapphire, Deel). Never link-spam; the names do the work.
- **Conversational filler** — *"Here's the thing"*, *"Let's be real"*, *"Truth bomb"*. Light. One per piece, max.
- **"Real-life examples"** framing — promised in intros, delivered later.

### Structural patterns

- **H1 = Title.** No second H1. Single H1 per piece.
- **H2 = Section.** Numbered lists ("Top 10", "14 Hacks") sit under an H2 with a colon or short tag.
- **H3 = Item.** Each list entry gets a short H3 + 1-paragraph rationale + bullet sub-points. Pattern:
  ```
  ## Top 10 Multi-Currency Credit Cards
  Chase Sapphire Preferred
  H3 (item name)
  Body paragraph — what it is + why it matters.
  Bullets — perks, fees, ideal user.
  ```
- **Lead magnet close** — every long piece ends with a soft CTA or "key takeaway" box.
- **No table of contents** in posts under 2,500 words.

### What's deliberately NOT in the voice

- ❌ "In this article, we will explore…" filler intros
- ❌ Hedging weasel words ("might", "could possibly", "some say")
- ❌ Academic / corporate passive voice
- ❌ Emoji in body copy (allowed in H3 / pull quotes only)
- ❌ Generic "It's important to note that…" sentences
- ❌ Royalty-free stock photo phrasing

## Citation & Linking Style

- **Inline brand mentions** without links: *"Chase Sapphire Preferred"*, *"Wise"*.
- **First-mention linking** for non-obvious tools: link the canonical URL once when first introduced.
- **No "according to [Source]" footnoting** — research is folded into prose.
- **Stats sourced inline** with parenthetical year: *"(as of 2025)"*, *"(Q1 2025 data)"*.

## Hook & Closing Templates

### Openers (pick one, never repeat in consecutive pieces)
1. **Vivid scene** — set a place + sensory detail in the first sentence.
2. **Money-frame** — open with a specific dollar/euro saving.
3. **Counterintuitive claim** — *"Geoarbitrage sounds like cheating, but it's just math."*

### Closers
1. **"Bottom line"** — one-paragraph summary, 3–5 bullets.
2. **Actionable next step** — "Open a Wise account today" or "Run your numbers with our calculator".
3. **Soft sign-off** — *"Now stop reading and go book that flight."*

## Tone Anchors (use these as in-context prompts)

> "Write like you're explaining a money hack to a smart friend at a hostel bar — knowledgeable, candid, no condescension, and never boring."

> "Lead with the punchline (the saving, the benefit, the counterintuitive truth), then back it up with specifics."

> "Snark is fine at institutions. Never at the reader."
