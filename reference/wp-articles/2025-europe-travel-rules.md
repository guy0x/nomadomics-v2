If you’ve been dreaming of a Euro trip in 2025, there’s a new set of rules you need to know before booking your trip. Starting soon, the European Union is rolling out two major systems that will change how non-Europeans travel to and within the Schengen Area: the Entry/Exit System (EES) and the European Travel Information and Authorization System (ETIAS).



These changes are designed to enhance border security and streamline entry processes, but they also mean a few extra steps for travelers. Don’t worry, though — here’s everything you need to know to navigate these new requirements like a pro, save time, and avoid unnecessary stress.



What Are EES and ETIAS?



EES was launched on November 10th, 2024 and you can think of it as Europe’s new digital bouncer. This automated system creates a record of your passport and biometric data in order to track your comings and goings from Europe’s Schengen Area. 



ETIAS is set to launch in 2025 and is basically an application that visa-free passport holders must fill out before entering Schengen. After applying, they’ll run a background check and, once approved, you’ll be able to enter the Schengen Area visa-free as you normally would.



EES (Entry/Exit System):



EES is an automated system that will electronically record the entry, exit, and refusal of entry for non-EU nationals traveling to the Schengen Area for short stays (up to 90 days within a 180-day period). 



It replaces the old-school passport stamping process with biometric data collection, like fingerprints and facial scans. This means no more ink stamps in your passport, but also no sneaking in an extra day without the system noticing.



ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorization System):



ETIAS is essentially the new visa waiver program for travelers from visa-exempt countries. If you’re from the U.S., Canada, Australia, or one of about 60 other countries, you’ll need to apply for ETIAS authorization before your trip. 



It’s not a visa, but it’s a mandatory pre-screening process to ensure you’re not a security risk. Think of it as a quick background check before you’re allowed to enter Schengen.



Why Nomads Should Care about EES & ETIAS



As digital nomads, we thrive on flexibility and freedom. These new systems add a layer of bureaucracy to your travel plans, but they’re also designed to make border crossings smoother and more secure.




No More Surprises at the Border: With ETIAS, you’ll know in advance if you’re cleared to enter the Schengen Area. No more last-minute denials at the airport.



Streamlined Entry: EES will speed up border checks by automating the process, especially if you have a biometric passport.



Avoid Overstaying: The EES will track your days in the Schengen Area, helping you stick to the 90/180 day visa rules and avoid fines or bans.




How to Navigate the New Rules



Step 1: Check Your Eligibility




If you’re from a visa-exempt country (like the U.S., Canada, or Japan), you’ll need ETIAS authorization.



If you’re from a country that requires a visa, the ETIAS doesn’t apply to you, but the EES will still track your entry and exit.




Step 2: Apply for ETIAS




When to Apply: ETIAS is expected to launch six months after the EES goes live, which is currently projected for 2025. Apply as soon as the system is operational and at least a few weeks before your trip.



How to Apply: The application is online and takes about 10 minutes. You’ll need:

A valid passport



An email address



A credit or debit card to pay the €7 fee (free for travelers under 18 or over 70).





What to Expect: Most applications are approved within minutes, but some may take up to 96 hours if additional checks are needed. This will be valid for three years or until your passport expires, whichever is sooner.




Step 3: Prepare for EES at the Border




Biometric Data: Be ready to provide fingerprints and a facial scan at your first entry point into the Schengen Area.



Self-Service Kiosks: If you have a biometric passport, you can use automated kiosks at many airports to speed up the process.



Plan for Delays: During the initial rollout, there may be longer wait times as travelers and border officials adjust to the new system. Arrive early, especially during peak travel seasons.




Step 4: Track Your Stay



Use apps or tools to monitor your days in the Schengen Area. Overstaying, even by one day, can result in fines, deportation, or bans from re-entry. The EES will automatically calculate your remaining days, but it’s always good to double-check.



Common EES/ETIAS Mistakes to Avoid




Procrastinating on ETIAS: Don’t wait until the last minute to apply. If your application is flagged for additional checks, it could delay your travel plans.



Incorrect Information: Double-check your ETIAS application for typos or errors. Even a small mistake could lead to rejection.



Assuming ETIAS is a Visa: ETIAS is not a visa. If you need a visa to enter the Schengen Area, you’ll still need to apply for one separately.



Ignoring the 90/180 Rule: The EES will track your days, so don’t try to game the system. Overstaying can have serious consequences.




How to Save Money on ETIAS Application




Apply Early: Avoid last-minute application fees or expedited processing costs by applying for ETIAS well in advance.



Use Fee-Free Cards: Pay the €7 ETIAS fee with a credit card that doesn’t charge foreign transaction fees to save a few bucks.



Avoid Overstaying: The penalties for overstaying can be steep, ranging from fines to travel bans. Plan your trips carefully to avoid these costs.



Leverage Biometric Passports: If you don’t already have one, consider upgrading to a biometric passport. It’ll save you time at the border and make the EES process smoother.
